# Key Findings — Canadian Labour Force Analysis (2020–2025)

## National Overview
Canada's labour market showed remarkable resilience over the 
2020–2025 period. After unemployment spiked to 10.15% at the 
height of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, the national rate 
recovered steadily, bottoming out at 6.06% in 2023 before 
rising slightly to 6.94% in 2025 — still well below pandemic 
highs. As of 2025, the employment rate stands at 59.90% with 
a labour force participation rate of 65.27%, and 82.16% of 
employed Canadians hold full-time positions.

## Finding 1 — Youth Unemployment Remains a Structural Problem
Youth unemployment (ages 15–24) has consistently run at nearly
double the national rate across the entire observation period.
At its peak in 2020, youth unemployment hit 18.9% compared to
the national rate of 10.2% — a gap of 8.7 percentage points.
While both rates improved significantly through 2022–2023, 
youth unemployment has since re-accelerated to 13.4% in 2025,
matching its 2021 level, even as the overall rate stabilized 
at 6.9%. This persistent gap suggests structural barriers 
facing young Canadians in accessing stable employment — 
particularly relevant for workforce planning and youth-focused
policy interventions.

## Finding 2 — Prairie Provinces Lead Employment Recovery
Saskatchewan (63.88%) and Alberta (63.86%) recorded the 
highest provincial employment rates in 2025, outperforming 
the national average of 59.90% by approximately 4 percentage 
points. Ontario, Canada's most populous province, sits at 
60.05% — close to the national average — while Newfoundland 
and Labrador lags at 51.89%, the lowest of all provinces. 
This regional disparity points to the continued strength of 
resource and energy-sector driven economies in Western Canada.

## Finding 3 — Prime Working Age Group Dominates Employment
The 25–54 age group accounts for the largest share of 
employed Canadians, with men at 34.38% and women at 31.35% 
of total employment in that bracket. The 55+ group 
contributes 11.79% (men) and 9.59% (women), while the youth 
cohort (15–24) represents the smallest share. The consistent 
gender gap across all age groups — where men outpace women 
in employment share — warrants attention from equity-focused 
workforce planning teams.

## Finding 4 — Services Sector Drives Canada's Labour Market
The services-producing sector accounts for 79.14% of all 
employment in Canada, dwarfing the goods-producing sector 
at 20.86%. Public services is the single largest employer 
at ~49,798K positions, followed by Trade (~35,094K) and 
Professional services (~21,927K). Among tracked growth 
sectors (2020–2025), Finance & real estate and Construction 
showed the strongest upward trajectory, while Trade levelled 
off post-2023 and Public services showed relative stability.

## Implications for Policy & Planning
- Youth unemployment requires targeted intervention — the 
  13.4% rate in 2025 despite an overall recovery signals a 
  skills or access gap rather than a demand problem
- Regional employment disparities between Saskatchewan/Alberta 
  and Atlantic Canada suggest uneven recovery patterns 
  requiring province-specific workforce strategies
- The dominance of services-sector employment (79%) means 
  Canada's labour market is increasingly sensitive to 
  service-sector disruptions (e.g., automation, AI adoption)
